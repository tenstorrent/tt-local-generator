# Music prompt cards: review sheet for the first set

Date: 2026-10-28. Branch: `music-prompts`. Written for the operator's review (spec section 10, "Authoring and the review gate").

There are 23 cards: the 16 names in the operator's request, the post-rock and bluegrass vocal starter cards, and five new style cards
(funk, and the parents that artist cards point to: Berlin school, krautrock, electropop and jam band). Every `sound` was written fresh. Each card was checked against web sources
during writing (Wikipedia plus reviews or reference pages, named in the card's `notes`). Wikipedia is one of the two sources on almost every
card, so the second source matters; where the second source was only a search-result summary, the card says "via web search".

The three renderings in each row are for the input `"<name>-like song"`: MiniMax (caption+lyrics), Stable Audio (tags) and an unknown model (generic).

## Unverified cards: please check these closely

No card is marked `unverified`. Some claims inside verified cards came from my own knowledge and were not confirmed by the sources I read.
They are listed under "Things I was unsure about" below. Please read those first.

## Changes to the three starter cards

- **post-rock**: `sound` no longer says "Mostly instrumental"; it says "rock played for texture and atmosphere". "instrumental" was removed from
  `tags`, and "sung lead vocals" was removed from `avoid` (post-rock often has vocals used as texture).
- **tortoise**: "brushed drums" became "loose, jazz-inflected drums" plus marimba and extra percussion. Wikipedia describes three percussionists
  switching between drums, vibraphone and marimba, and none of my sources mentions brushes. The accepted example in spec section 5 said
  "brushed drums", so the golden tests now look for "jazz-inflected drums" instead. "instrumental" was removed from `tags`.
- **bluegrass-vocal**: `lead_instrument` changed from `pedal steel` to `dobro`. Pedal steel is a country instrument and is not part of the
  bluegrass lineup; the resonator guitar (dobro) is. The accepted Stable Audio example said "pedal steel"; the golden test now looks for
  "dobro". The rhythm now says the mandolin chops on beats two and four.

## Changes since the first sheet (fix round 1)

Card text:
- **Voices moved out of `sound`, `tags` and `instruments` into `vocal`.** The Sea and Cake, P-funk, Parliament, Funkadelic, Kraftwerk,
  Grateful Dead songbook, electropop, Yume Bitsu and synthpop. The `vocal` text is written only when the prompt sings (a vocal layer is asked
  for and the model can carry it); an instrumental prompt never shows it. Tags that named a voice were replaced: P-funk "group chants" ->
  "horn section", "cosmic groove"; Parliament "group chants" -> "clavinet"; Kraftwerk and electropop "vocoder" -> "drum machine",
  "precise"; Grateful Dead "three-part harmony" -> "folk rock". Parliament's harmony no longer says "vocal harmony".
- **Berlin school and Tangerine Dream:** "Mellotron choirs" became "Mellotron washes" and "Mellotron strings", and "string and choir pads"
  became "string pads", so an instrumental prompt has no "choir".
- **Synthpop:** "a singable chorus" moved to `vocal`; `sound` ends at "catchy synth riffs".
- **Ash Ra Tempel:** the organ and "early synthesizers" were not credited on the 1971 debut. The release notes for the 50th anniversary edition
  credit Göttsching with electric guitar, vocals and electronics and Schulze with drums, percussion and electronics, and the Cambridge Companion
  chapter describes delay-treated guitar and a distorted bass. Instruments are now delay-treated electric guitar, distorted bass guitar, drums
  and percussion, electronics; `sound` ends on "quiet electronic drones" in place of organ drones.
- **Yume Bitsu:** the notes no longer say "mostly instrumental"; Wikipedia credits Forkner with guitar and vocals. A soft, low vocal is in `vocal`.
- **Kraftwerk:** production "Clean, sparse and spacious." (the old wording rendered as "Clean, sparse and with lots of space").
- **Sources:** every card's `notes` now lists sources as "Publisher: title", with at least two different publishers. Cards that had only Wikipedia
  pages (funk, P-funk, Parliament, Funkadelic, krautrock, electropop, synthpop, jam band, Grateful Dead, accordion) gained a second publisher
  from a web search. Where I read only the search result's summary, the source says "search summary".

Behaviour that changes what the sheet shows:
- A Stable Audio prompt keeps a tag from every card the wand picks: each layer card is its own clause with its label and first three tags.
- "Tortoise", "Tortoise style" and "Tortoise!" on their own now match the Tortoise card.
- A typed "a" before a replaced name agrees with the style: "a Kraftwerk groove" -> "an electropop groove".
- The lead line for a model that cannot sing reads "plaintive dobro lead line carrying a bluegrass melody" (was "phrased like a bluegrass vocal").

Still awaiting the operator: the accepted example in spec section 5 says "brushed drums" and "pedal steel"; the cards say "jazz-inflected drums"
and "dobro" (see "Changes to the three starter cards" above). The spec is unchanged.

## Things I was unsure about

Tempo ranges. No source I read gave BPM figures for any style. Every `tempo_bpm` range is my own estimate:
post-rock 70-130, Tortoise 80-125, Yume Bitsu 70-115, The Sea and Cake 95-135, bebop 160-280, funk 90-120, P-funk 90-115,
Parliament 95-115, Funkadelic 80-120, Berlin school and Tangerine Dream 100-130, krautrock 110-150, Ash Ra Tempel 70-120,
electropop and Kraftwerk 100-130, chiptunes 120-180, synthpop 105-135, jam band 85-140, Grateful Dead 80-140,
accordion waltz 76-96, bagpipe slow air 60-90, bluegrass vocal 100-160.

Other claims I could not confirm from the sources:
- **Bebop**: the head played "in unison" by trumpet and alto sax, and sparse piano comping. Wikipedia confirms head-solos-head, walking bass
  and ride cymbal with bass-drum accents, but not unison or comping.
- **Funkadelic**: fuzz and wah guitar. The sources say "guitar-driven psychedelic rock" and name Hendrix as an influence, but do not name the effects.
- **Parliament**: "Moog synth bass" is confirmed for "Flash Light" and "Aqua Boogie" on the P-Funk page; I applied it to the band's sound in general.
  "Choirs of chanting voices" follows "intricate horn and vocal arrangements"; the word "chants" is mine.
- **Yume Bitsu**: few sources describe the band in detail (the Wikipedia page is short and flagged for citations). "Delay-soaked guitars",
  "keyboard drones" and "rolling drums" are my reading of "dreamy, swirling guitar sound" and the band's instrument list. How much of their
  music has vocals I could not establish; the card puts a soft, low vocal in `vocal`.
- **Jam band**: the second publisher (Saving Country Music) reviews a Grateful Dead album; no general source on jam bands besides Wikipedia.
- **The Sea and Cake**: "major-seventh and suspended chords" is my own description of the harmony. The breathy vocal and bossa nova lilt are confirmed.
- **Ash Ra Tempel**: "fuzz-heavy freak-outs" is my description. The delay-treated guitar and distorted bass come from a search summary of the
  Cambridge Companion chapter. The instruments follow the 1971 debut's credits; later albums (Join Inn, Seven Up) may differ.
- **Berlin school**: "kosmische musik" and "kosmische" are aliases of this card. In common use the term also means krautrock in general; in the
  narrow sense it means 1970s German synthesizer music, which is closer to this card. Please decide where it belongs.
- **Electropop**: Kraftwerk's parent card. Wikipedia treats electropop as a harder-edged variant of synth-pop and names Kraftwerk as its
  "godfathers", but it dates the genre to the early 1980s. "Robot pop" (Kraftwerk's own phrase) is an alias.
- **Kraftwerk**: "deadpan, half-spoken vocals" comes from reviews ("robotized vocalization", "vocoder-lined vocals"); Wikipedia does not describe the delivery.
- **Synthpop**: gated-reverb drums are a 1980s production trait I know of; the Synth-pop article does not mention them.
- **Ratcheting synths**: the link to Tangerine Dream (inspiration) comes from synth-forum and blog sources. I found no reference work that states it.
- **Grateful Dead songbook**: modelled as an artist card under a new `jam-band` style, family `jam`, so the wand can pair it with the bluegrass
  vocal (same-family cards are never paired). "Mixolydian runs" comes from the Wikipedia note on Garcia's playing.
- **Accordion in the moonlight**: the slow-waltz tempo is my estimate. The musette tremolo tuning is not in the card because my source did not cover it.
- **Bagpipes on the beach at sunset**: Wikipedia says the Highland pipes have no dynamic control; it does not call them loud, so the card does not either.

Matching decisions you may want to change:
- **"funk" and "Parliament" are not marked ambiguous.** The spec names "funk" as an ambiguous word. Marking it ambiguous would make plain
  "funk" or "Parliament" typed alone match nothing (an ambiguous single word needs a cue, a "-like" form, or a capital in mid-sentence), and
  both are names the operator typed. The cost: "a song about being in a funk" will pick up the funk card.
- **"bop" is not an alias of bebop.** "Bop" is a real short name for bebop, and it is also current slang for a catchy song.
- **An ambiguous name matches when the text is plainly a request for it** (fix round 1): typed alone ("Tortoise", "Tortoise style",
  "some Tortoise"), or anywhere in a text where another card matched ("post-rock, tortoise"). The last rule means "the tortoise crossed the
  road, post-rock" now matches the Tortoise card too. A sentence with no other card ("the tortoise crossed the road") still does not.
- **A voice-defined card on its own renders as an instrumental** ("Kraftwerk-like" on MiniMax has no vocals and no lyrics). Its voice is
  described only when the request has a vocal ("Kraftwerk-like but someone is singing about trains on top"). If you want "Kraftwerk-like" alone
  to sing, MiniMax also needs lyrics; that is a translator decision, not a card change.
